Your two goals
①
Reach an MCI of 0.65
The Material Circularity Indicator (Ellen MacArthur Foundation / Granta) summarises how circular your product really is, on a 0.1–1.0 scale. You start near 0.18.
②
Maintain profitability
Year-5 operating profit must be at least your Year-0 baseline of $420M. Circularity that bankrupts the company teaches the wrong lesson.
⚠ It is not meant to be easy. Most strategies hit one goal at the expense of the other. Expect to play several times: impact levels are hidden, the industry-average lifespan rises each year, and not every initiative does what its marketing suggests — so standing still or chasing surface-level wins both mean falling behind.
The central tension

Longer-lasting phones sell fewer phones.

The single biggest circularity lever is product lifespan — but every extra year of life slows replacement demand and erodes hardware revenue. Roughly 80% of a smartphone's lifecycle carbon is locked in at manufacturing, so making fewer, longer-lived devices is exactly what cuts emissions — and exactly what threatens the traditional sales model.

The way through is the same lesson the fashion version teaches: growth has to come from new circular business models — trade-in and refurbishment, repair services, battery programs, and device-as-a-service — not from shipping more units.

How your score is computed

This simulation uses the actual Material Circularity Indicator method, evaluated each year for one representative device:

V = M·(1 − FR − FU)
W = M·(1−C) + ½·[ WF + WC ]
LFI = (V + W) / 2M
X = L / Lavg  (utility)
MCI = max( 0 , 1 − LFI · 0.9/X )

where FR=recycled input %, FU=reused/remanufactured %, C=collection rate, E=recycling efficiency (inside W), L=actual device lifespan, Lavg=industry-average lifespan. A fully linear product with average utility scores 0.1; a fully circular one scores 1.0.
